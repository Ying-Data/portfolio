The problem you are actually hiring for

You are not hiring an analyst because you lack dashboards. You have dashboards. You are hiring one because decisions still get made on a gut feeling in a hallway, five metres from a report that answered the wrong question.

I spent eight years on the other side of that hallway, holding a EUR 2M client book at 95% retention and helping grow EU revenue 40%. When a report was late or wrong, the consequences did not land on the analyst. They landed on me.

Then I moved to Belgium and started over. I am a Cantonese speaker who learned Dutch and ended up inside a Belgian accountancy practice, where paper invoices lived in ExpertM and digital ones in Odoo, while the BTW deadline had no interest in the difference. I got tired of waiting for other people's reports to tell me what I already suspected. So I taught myself to build them, 1,000+ hours at BeCode deep, until the numbers I published were numbers I could defend.

Most analysts learn the model first and then go looking for a business to point it at. I ran the business first. That order is harder to fake, and it means you will not spend my first quarter explaining what a margin is.

If any of that sounds like your Monday, keep scrolling. Everything below is proof, not promises.

Step 1, the disagreement

Two systems, one set of money

Paper invoices were keyed into ExpertM. Digital invoices flowed into Odoo. Every month they disagreed: same suppliers spelled three ways, duplicates, a payment with no reference. Finding out why took two days of side-by-side checking.

Step 2, reconcile the easy money

Exact matches first, no judgment calls

Reference, supplier and amount agree on both sides: matched and set aside. This clears most of the volume in seconds and, more importantly, gets it out of the way of the rows that need attention.

Step 3, reconcile the stubborn rows

Fuzzy matches, each with its reason on the label

The remainder pairs on rules a reviewer can audit: same amount, supplier name variant. Same amount, date shifted. Nothing matches silently; every pairing carries the reason it was allowed.

Step 4, reduce it to what needs a human

What is left is the real work

A cash payment with no reference and a duplicate entry stay in the exception queue, flagged for a human decision. A reconciliation that claims one hundred percent is hiding something. This one shows its residue.

Step 5, read it in one minute

One page a partner reads in a minute

The output is not a spreadsheet, it is a decision: what matched, what matched with a reason, what needs you, and a difference of 0,00 once the exceptions are resolved. The cycle that took two days now takes two hours, and the checking became repeatable instead of heroic.
