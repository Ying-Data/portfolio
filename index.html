02How I think

For eight years I was the person on the hook for the number. In international business, across sales, supply chain, trade and finance, I carried a EUR 2M client book at 95% retention and helped grow EU revenue by 40%. That is where I learned how a business actually makes money, which question is worth asking, and why a metric moved.

Then I got tired of waiting on other people's reports, so I went and built the skill myself. Most analysts learn the model first and then look for a business to point it at. I did it the other way round, business first and model second, which is the harder order to fake and the one that needs less ramp time.

My method Question to Decision
1

Start from your business question and the person on your team who has to act on it.

2

Model only what that decision needs, nothing built to impress.

3

Build the SQL and the Power BI once, and build it right, so your team gets a refresh, not a rebuild.

4

Hand back a recommendation in plain language a non-technical manager can act on without me in the room.

The tool is table stakes. What you hire me for is the recommendation at the end.

The certifications, for the box-tickers: BeCode AI and ML bootcamp (1,000+ hours), AWS Cloud Practitioner, 2nd at the Accenture hackathon (MySQL to Tableau), BA International Business (Coventry, UK). Real, and the part of the page that matters least.

03Beyond the numbers

Why my numbers get acted on

The part that makes the analytics land is trained, not a personality quirk. Here is where.

I do stand-up comedy

On a stage, to strangers who will not pretend to laugh. It is the same muscle that turns a dashboard into a decision: read the room, cut what does not land, say the one thing that does in words a tired person gets the first time. Bomb on stage and you find out instantly. Ship a report nobody reads and you find out in three months.

Toastmasters, VP of PR

I run public relations for Toastmasters Belgium, so I spend my evenings helping nervous people make a clear point to a room. That is most of what BI actually is once the SQL runs: getting a non-technical manager to see the thing and act on it.

I chase good food

Mostly noodles, across whichever city will have me. Same instinct as the work: go find the real thing, ignore the version that just looks good in the photo.
